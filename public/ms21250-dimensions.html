    MS21250 Bolt Chart & Dimensions

    Part-Number Decoder, Diameter Codes and a Grip-Coverage Matrix

    An MS21250 part number encodes the bolt's diameter and grip length in two number fields. This chart decodes the full callout, gives the verified diameter codes, explains the one offset that trips people up on odd grip dashes, and lays out a coverage matrix of the grip lengths commonly stocked for each diameter. Where a value has not been verified against the controlling drawing, the decoder rule is given instead of a guessed number, and per-size load tables in pounds are deliberately left off because no public source carries them cleanly.

    Decoding MS21250[H]-DD-LLL

    The callout has three parts after the base number. The optional H directly after MS21250 marks a drilled head for safety wire; with no H the head is undrilled. DD is the diameter dash, the shank diameter counted in sixteenths of an inch. LLL is the grip length, the unthreaded shank under the head, also counted in sixteenths. So MS21250H04004 is a 1/4-inch drilled-head bolt with a 0.250-inch grip, and MS21250-05022 is a 5/16-inch undrilled bolt with a grip read from code 022.

    FieldMeaningWorked value
    MS21250Base standard numberMS21250
    H (optional)Drilled head for safety wireMS21250H04004
    DDDiameter in 1/16" units04 = 1/4", 05 = 5/16"
    LLLGrip length in 1/16" units008 = 0.500"

    Worked in full: MS21250-04008 is a 1/4-inch bolt with a 0.500-inch grip; MS21250H04004 is the same diameter, drilled head, 0.250-inch grip; MS21250-05022 is a 5/16-inch bolt whose grip code is 022. The matching strength class and thread form sit on the specification page.

    The grip rule and the odd-dash offset

    For even grip codes the rule is the clean one: divide the code by 16 to get the length in inches. Code 008 is 0.500 inch, code 016 is 1.000 inch, code 024 is 1.500 inch. The base step is 0.0625 inch, one sixteenth, per increment.

    The detail almost nobody states is what happens on odd grip dashes. Odd codes carry a small +0.061-inch offset on top of the even-code value, so code 022 lands at about 1.375 inch rather than exactly 22 × 0.0625. The clean divide-by-16 rule is exact for even codes and a close working figure for odd ones; for a tight fit on an odd grip, take the dimension from the controlling drawing rather than the arithmetic. The table shows the even-code conversion that the rule covers exactly.

    Diameter dash codes

    For most of the range the diameter dash equals the diameter in sixteenths, so dash 08 is 8/16 (1/2) inch and dash 14 is 14/16 (7/8) inch. The verified rows below carry exact callouts. The small-diameter codes do not all fall on a clean sixteenth: dash 03 is 0.190 inch, the #10 screw size, not 3/16 inch, which is the single correction most listings get wrong. For the unverified middle rows the decoder rule (diameter equals DD divided by 16) gives the working figure, but the exact small-size callout maps to the nearest standard screw size and should be confirmed against NASM21250 before a controlled order.

    The "dash equals diameter in sixteenths" rule is a quick sanity check for dash 04 and up: if a quoted diameter and dash do not satisfy it, one of them has been transcribed wrongly. Dash 03 is the exception, because the #10 screw size at 0.190 inch does not sit on a clean sixteenth. The full part-number logic and the strength class sit on the specification page.

    Grip-coverage matrix by diameter

    Reading the matrix is the same two-step every time: pick the diameter dash on the left, then pick a grip dash from its row and divide by 16 for the length. The 1/4-inch and 5/16-inch diameters carry the widest grip ranges because they are the most commonly used sizes; the larger diameters are stocked in fewer grips. To turn any cell into a full part number, prefix it with MS21250 (or MS21250H for a drilled head), as in MS21250-05022 for the 5/16-inch diameter at grip 22.

    How to choose the grip length

    Selecting the grip is a measurement task, not a guess. Measure the total thickness of the parts the bolt clamps, from the bearing face under the flange to the face the nut seats against. Convert that thickness to sixteenths and pick the grip dash at or just above it, so the unthreaded shank spans the joint and the thread begins past the clamped material. For a 0.700-inch stack, grip 012 (0.750 inch) is the natural choice; for a 0.950-inch stack, grip 016 (1.000 inch) fits. Keeping the thread out of the clamped zone is what lets the bolt hold an accurate, repeatable preload, which is the whole point of a tension fastener.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I read an MS21250 dash number?

    The first dash field is the diameter in sixteenths of an inch, so dash 05 is 5/16 inch. The second field is the grip length, also in sixteenths, so 012 is 0.750 inch.

    Is MS21250 dash 03 the same as 3/16 inch?

    No. Dash 03 is 0.190 inch, the #10 screw size, not 3/16 inch (0.1875). It is the one small-diameter code that does not sit on a clean sixteenth, and it is widely mis-stated as 3/16 inch online.

    Why does grip 022 work out to 1.375 inch and not exactly 22/16?

    Odd grip dashes carry a small +0.061-inch offset above the even-code value, so code 022 lands at about 1.375 inch. The clean divide-by-16 rule is exact for even codes; for a tight odd-grip fit, take the dimension from the controlling drawing.

    Which MS21250 sizes are commonly stocked?

    The 1/4-inch (dash 04) and 5/16-inch (dash 05) diameters are stocked across the widest grip ranges, while larger diameters such as 5/8 and 7/8 inch are held in only a few grips. The grip-coverage matrix above lists the commonly stocked grip dash codes for each diameter.
