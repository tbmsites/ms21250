    MS21250 Specification

    Bolt, Tension, Steel, External Wrenching, Flanged, 12-Point

    This page sets out the MS21250 specification in plain terms: what the standard covers, the material and strength it calls for, how its part numbers are built, and how it relates to NASM21250 and the MIL-B-8831 procurement specification. Figures here are drawn from the specification index and the controlling drawing; where public sources disagree, the disputed value is left out rather than stated as fact.

    What is MS21250?

    MS21250 is a United States Military Standard for a high-strength bolt. Its full title is "Bolt, Tension, Steel, External Wrenching, Flanged, 12-Point." In words: it is a steel tension bolt with a flanged head that has twelve external wrenching points (a double-hex drive), designed so a socket can apply high torque without rounding the head. The standard has since been redesignated NASM21250 under industry stewardship, and the bolt is procured against the specification MIL-B-8831B. A buyer who calls out MS21250 and one who calls out NASM21250 are asking for the same fastener.

    It helps to take the title apart, because every word is a requirement. "Bolt" and "tension" together say the part is meant to be loaded along its axis and held in tension by a nut or tapped hole, not used as a shear pin. "Steel" fixes the material class. "External wrenching" means the driving surfaces are on the outside of the head, gripped by a socket, rather than recessed inside the head like a socket-cap screw. "Flanged" adds an integral collar under the head that spreads the bearing load. "12-point" describes the drive pattern: a double hexagon with twelve flats. Read as a whole, the title specifies a steel tension bolt with a wide-bearing flanged head that a socket can grip at twelve points.

    The standard belongs to the family of U.S. military aerospace and machinery hardware sheets. Each MS sheet defines one part configuration and its size range, and points to a procurement specification for the material and acceptance rules. For MS21250 that procurement specification is MIL-B-8831. Knowing this two-document structure is the key to reading the standard: the MS sheet answers "what shape and what sizes", and MIL-B-8831 answers "made of what, to what strength, and tested how".

    What material and strength does MS21250 specify?

    MS21250 bolts are made from alloy steel per the governing specification and are cadmium plated. The key mechanical requirement is a minimum ultimate tensile strength of 180,000 psi (180 ksi). The bolt is rated for service up to 450°F. The alloy steel is heat treated to reach that strength, and each lot is verified against the controlling drawing's per-size tensile and shear minimums, which are expressed as a minimum load in pounds rather than a single stress figure. Buyers needing the exact heat-treat condition or plating callout for a given order should confirm it against the MIL-B-8831 procurement specification.

    A short word on why the strength is given as a load rather than a hardness or a single shear stress. A bolt's job is to carry a load, so the controlling drawing lists, for each diameter, the minimum tensile load and the minimum double-shear load the part must withstand in pounds. Those load minimums already account for the bolt's section and thread, so an inspector can pull-test a sample and read the result straight against the table. This site reproduces the verified diameter and grip codes on the bolt chart; it does not publish a single shear stress figure for MS21250, because public sources do not agree on one and a wrong number on a structural fastener page would be worse than none.

    The 180 ksi class places MS21250 among the higher-strength threaded fasteners. The alloy steel reaches that level through heat treatment rather than through cold work, which is what allows the bolt to keep its strength at elevated temperature up to its 450°F rating. The cadmium plating is a corrosion finish; it is thin enough not to interfere with thread fit but should be considered when a joint has galvanic or temperature constraints. For the precise chemistry and heat-treat condition, the governing document is MIL-B-8831, covered on the MIL-B-8831 page.

    How do you read an MS21250 part number?

    An MS21250 part number is built as MS21250 [H] – DD – LLL. The optional H immediately after the base number marks a drilled head for safety wire; without it the head is undrilled. DD is the diameter dash code that sets the nominal shank size. LLL is the grip length expressed in sixteenths of an inch. Worked example: MS21250-05012 is a 5/16-inch diameter bolt with a 12/16-inch (0.750-inch) grip; add the H, as in MS21250H05012, and the same bolt has a drilled head.

    Two habits make the part number easy to read. First, treat the diameter dash as a count of sixteenths: dash 05 is five sixteenths, which is 5/16 inch. Second, treat the grip code the same way: code 012 is twelve sixteenths, which is 0.750 inch. Because both fields use the same sixteenth-inch unit, a single mental conversion handles the whole part number. The grip is the unthreaded length under the head, so it should be chosen to match the thickness of the parts being clamped, leaving the thread clear of the joint. The full set of diameter codes and a grip conversion table are on the MS21250 bolt chart.

    Is MS21250 the same as NASM21250?

    Yes. NASM21250 is the redesignation of MS21250. When U.S. defense fastener standards were moved to industry control, the MS sheet was reissued under the NASM prefix and the dimensional and mechanical requirements were retained. The change is administrative: the bolt described by NASM21250 has the same head geometry, the same 180 ksi minimum tensile strength and the same part-number logic as the bolt described by MS21250. Either callout buys the same part, and both trace back to the MIL-B-8831 procurement specification.

    The 12-point flanged head

    The head is what sets this bolt apart from a plain hex. A 12-point, or double-hex, head has two hexagons offset by thirty degrees, giving twelve flats for a socket to bear on. Compared with a six-point hex of the same strength, the twelve-point pattern lets a wrench re-engage at thirty-degree steps instead of sixty, which is useful in tight spaces where a full wrench swing is impossible. Because the load is shared over twelve points, the head can be turned to high torque with less risk of rounding the corners.

    The flange is the second feature. It is an integral collar formed under the head that increases the bearing diameter, so the clamp load is spread over a wider area of the joint face. That reduces the bearing stress under the head and can remove the need for a separate washer in some joints. Taken together, the wrenching points and the flange are what make MS21250 a tension fastener built for high, repeatable preload rather than a general-purpose bolt. Head and flange dimensions follow the diameter, which is selected from the codes on the bolt chart page.

    Frequently asked questions

    What does the MS in MS21250 stand for?

    MS denotes Military Standard. MS21250 is the standard sheet that defines the 12-point flanged tension bolt; it has since been redesignated NASM21250.

    What is the tensile strength of an MS21250 bolt?

    The minimum ultimate tensile strength is 180,000 psi (180 ksi). Per-size acceptance is checked against the minimum tensile and shear loads in pounds listed on the controlling drawing.

    What does the H in MS21250H mean?

    The H marks a drilled head, which lets the bolt be safety wired. Without the H the head is undrilled.

    What material is an MS21250 bolt made from?

    It is made from heat-treated alloy steel per the governing specification and is cadmium plated. The exact alloy and heat-treat condition are controlled by the MIL-B-8831 procurement specification rather than by the MS sheet itself.
